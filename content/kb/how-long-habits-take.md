# How long it really takes to build a habit

The "21 days to form a habit" line is everywhere, and it is not supported by research. If you expected a habit to feel effortless after three weeks and it doesn't, nothing is wrong with you. You are simply early.

## What the research found

In 2010, a team at University College London asked 96 volunteers to pick one new daily behaviour, such as drinking a glass of water with lunch or running for 15 minutes before dinner. Every day for 12 weeks, participants rated how automatic the behaviour felt.

For the people whose data fit the model well, automaticity rose quickly at first and then levelled off. The time to reach that plateau had a **median of 66 days**, and individual estimates ranged from **18 to 254 days**. Some of the longer figures are projections beyond the 12-week study, so treat them as rough, but the message is clear: weeks to months, not days.

## Why the range is so wide

- **The behaviour matters.** Drinking water was quicker to automate than doing 50 sit-ups. Simple actions settle in faster than effortful ones.
- **The cue matters.** Behaviours tied to a reliable trigger ("after breakfast") become automatic more easily than ones that float around the day.
- **People differ.** Schedules, motivation and environment all change the curve.

## The encouraging part: early gains are the biggest

The habit curve is steepest at the start. The first few weeks of consistent repetition deliver most of the progress in automaticity; later repetitions add less each time. That means the effort you put in early pays off disproportionately, and it also means the "hard" phase does not last forever.

## What this means for your haGIT streaks

- **Judge a habit after two to three months, not two weeks.** If it still takes effort at day 20, that is normal.
- **Shrink the habit if it feels heavy.** A smaller, simpler version reaches automaticity faster, and you can grow it later.
- **Anchor it to a fixed moment.** "After I pour my morning coffee, I review my plan" gives your brain a consistent cue to learn.
- **Watch the trend, not single days.** Your contribution graph will show the shape of the curve better than any one square.

## Key takeaway

Building a habit is closer to a two-month project than a three-week challenge. Expect it to feel effortful for a while, keep the behaviour small and cued, and let repetition do its slow work.
