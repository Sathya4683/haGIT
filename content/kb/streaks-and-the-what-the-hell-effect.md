# Streaks and the "what-the-hell" effect

Streaks are one of the most motivating features of habit apps. They are also one of the most fragile. Understanding both sides helps you use them well.

## Streaks motivate

Marketing researchers Jackie Silverman and Alixandra Barasch ran seven studies on how streaks shown in activity logs affect behaviour. When people's logs highlighted an **intact streak**, they were more likely to keep doing the behaviour than when the same history was presented as a **broken streak**. Remarkably, the effect depended on how the behaviour was *represented*, not just on how much people had actually done. Keeping a streak alive becomes a goal in itself.

## Broken streaks can backfire

The same research found that a broken streak can reduce engagement, especially when people blame the break on themselves rather than on circumstances. The effect was weaker when people could "repair" the streak.

This resembles what psychologists call the **"what-the-hell" effect**, first described in dieting research by Janet Polivy and Peter Herman: once a self-imposed rule is broken, people often abandon it entirely ("I've already ruined today, so...") instead of limiting the damage.

## How to keep streaks working for you

- **Track more than the current streak.** Your longest streak and your consistency over the last month are better measures of a habit. A 26-out-of-30 month is excellent, even with a broken streak in the middle.
- **Make the minimum easy.** If keeping the streak only requires the two-minute version, you'll protect it on hard days. (See *Start tiny*.)
- **Attribute breaks fairly.** Illness, travel and emergencies are external. Treat them as such instead of as a judgement on your character.
- **Repair quickly.** The day after a break is the most important day. (See *Never miss twice*.)
- **Use a reminder before the day ends.** A nightly nudge about habits that are still open is a gentle way to protect a streak without obsessing over it.

## Key takeaway

Streaks are powerful motivators, so use them, but don't let a single break flip you into "what the hell" mode. Measure consistency, keep the minimum tiny, and get back on track the very next day.
