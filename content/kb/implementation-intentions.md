# When X happens, I will do Y

Most goals are written as outcomes: "exercise more", "read every day". The trouble is that an outcome doesn't tell you *what to do at 7:40 pm on a Tuesday*. Implementation intentions fix that gap.

## What an implementation intention is

An implementation intention is a plan in **if-then** form that links a specific situation to a specific action:

> **When** [situation], **I will** [behaviour].

- "When I sit down at my desk after lunch, I will review my flashcards for five minutes."
- "When my alarm goes off, I will drink a glass of water before checking my phone."
- "When I close my laptop for the day, I will commit my progress in haGIT."

## How well it works

Psychologists Peter Gollwitzer and Paschal Sheeran pooled 94 independent tests of if-then planning. People who formed implementation intentions reached their goals substantially more often than people who only set the goal, with an average effect of **d = 0.65**, which is a medium-to-large effect by social-science standards. (That number is not "65% more success"; it is a standardised measure of how far apart the groups were.)

The benefits showed up at several stages: starting on time, staying on track when distracted, and recovering after a setback.

## Why it works

1. **The cue becomes "hot".** Once you have linked a situation to an action, your attention picks up that situation more readily.
2. **The decision is pre-made.** When the moment arrives, you don't have to deliberate; you just follow the plan. That saves willpower for when you need it.
3. **It bridges intention and action.** Most failures happen not because people don't want the goal, but because the right moment passes unnoticed.

## Writing good if-then plans

- **Make the "when" concrete and observable.** "After I brush my teeth at night" beats "in the evening".
- **Make the "then" small and specific.** "Write one paragraph" beats "work on my essay".
- **Plan for obstacles too.** "If I'm too tired to run, then I will walk for ten minutes." Obstacle plans are where if-then thinking really shines.
- **Write it down.** Say it out loud or put it in your habit's first commit message.

## Using it with haGIT

Name the cue in your habit itself, or keep a pinned commit message such as "Plan: after lunch → 10 min Spanish". When the coach notices a weak weekday, turn it into an if-then plan for that specific day.

## Key takeaway

Don't just decide *what* you want to do. Decide *when and where* you will do it, in one sentence: "When X, I will Y." It is a small step with one of the best evidence records in behaviour science.
