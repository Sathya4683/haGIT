# The fresh start effect

"I'll start on Monday" sounds like procrastination. Research suggests there is something real behind it.

## What the research found

Hengchen Dai, Katherine Milkman and Jason Riis looked at three kinds of real-world data:

- **Google searches for "diet"** were higher at the start of a new year, and also at the start of each week and month and after public holidays.
- **Gym visits** at a university fitness centre rose at the start of a week, at the start of a semester, and right after people's birthdays.
- **Goal commitments** on a commitment website spiked after similar landmarks.

The authors call these moments **temporal landmarks**. They argue that landmarks open a new "mental accounting period": past failures feel like they belonged to an older version of you, and you think about the bigger picture of your life. Both make aspirational behaviour more appealing. (The data are observational, so they show a pattern rather than proving cause, but the pattern was consistent across datasets.)

## Using fresh starts on purpose

- **Pick a landmark for a new habit.** The first of the month, the start of a semester, a birthday, or simply next Monday.
- **Create your own landmarks.** A new project, moving desks, or returning from a trip can all feel like a new chapter.
- **Restart after a slump.** If a habit slipped, a landmark is a natural moment to recommit, without dragging the guilt of the last few weeks along.
- **Prepare before the day.** Fresh-start motivation is a spark. Use it to set up the cue, the if-then plan and the tiny version so the habit doesn't depend on the spark alone.

## A caution

Fresh starts can become an excuse to delay ("I'll start next month"). If today is a good day to begin, begin today with the smallest version, and use the next landmark to level up. Waiting for a landmark is best for *restarts* and *bigger changes*, not for tiny habits you could start right now.

## In haGIT

The start of a week or month is a good time to run a weekly review with the coach, prune habits that no longer matter, and create a fresh branch for the next experiment. Your contribution graph keeps your past; the new month is a clean column.

## Key takeaway

Moments that feel like new beginnings boost motivation. Use Mondays, new months and personal milestones to start or restart, and convert that motivation into a concrete plan before it fades.
