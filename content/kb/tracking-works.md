# Tracking works

If you are logging habits in haGIT, you are already using one of the better-evidenced tools for change. Here is what the research says, and how to get the most from it.

## The evidence

Benjamin Harkin and colleagues analysed **138 randomised studies with nearly 20,000 participants** in which some people were prompted to monitor their progress toward a goal and others were not. Monitoring interventions improved goal attainment with an average effect of **d = 0.40**, a solid small-to-medium effect. They also found that the more people actually monitored their progress, the more it helped.

Two features made monitoring work better:

1. **Physically recording the information** (writing it down, logging it) rather than just thinking about it.
2. **Reporting or making the progress public**, for example sharing it with others.

## Why monitoring helps

- **It closes the feedback loop.** You can't steer toward a goal you aren't measuring.
- **It reveals patterns.** "I always skip Fridays" is invisible until you look at the data.
- **It makes progress visible**, which is motivating in itself.
- **It creates a moment of reflection** each time you log.

## Getting more out of your tracking

- **Log right after the behaviour.** A commit at the moment of action is more accurate and doubles as a small celebration.
- **Write a meaningful message.** "Read 15 pages of *Deep Work*" tells you more later than "done".
- **Review weekly.** Look at your contribution graph and per-habit stats once a week. The weekly review from the haGIT coach, or the weekly email report, is a good ritual.
- **Track the behaviour, not just the outcome.** "Studied 30 minutes" is something you control; "got an A" isn't.
- **Consider sharing.** Telling a friend or an accountability partner about your weekly numbers adds the "public" ingredient from the research.

## Watch out for

- **Tracking fatigue.** Track a few habits well rather than many habits badly.
- **All-or-nothing thinking.** Tracking should inform you, not punish you. A missed day is data. (See *Never miss twice*.)

## Key takeaway

Monitoring your progress measurably improves your chances of reaching a goal, especially when you record it and share it. Log consistently, write useful notes, and look back regularly.
