# Temptation bundling

Some habits pay off later (exercise, studying, cleaning) while temptations pay off now (a gripping audiobook, a favourite show, a fancy coffee). Temptation bundling joins the two: you only get the treat while you are doing the habit.

## The gym and the audiobooks

Katherine Milkman, Julia Minson and Kevin Volpp tested this with 226 gym members. One group could listen to tempting audiobooks (such as *The Hunger Games*) **only on iPods kept at the gym**. Another group was encouraged to save their audiobooks for the gym, on the honour system. A control group got no bundling.

Early in the study, people with gym-only audiobooks visited the gym **51% more often** than the control group, and the honour-system group about **29% more**. The effects shrank over time, especially after the Thanksgiving break. At the end, 61% of participants said they would pay to keep gym-only access to tempting audiobooks.

Two lessons: bundling gives a real boost, and it works best combined with other habit supports rather than as the only strategy.

## Why it works

- **It adds an immediate reward** to a behaviour whose rewards are delayed.
- **It reduces the "cost" of starting**, because you are looking forward to part of the session.
- **The restriction matters.** The treat is special because you can't have it elsewhere. That is why the strict version beat the honour system.

## Ideas for bundles

| Habit you avoid | Treat you love |
|---|---|
| Running or walking | A podcast or audiobook series you only hear while moving |
| Household chores | A favourite playlist |
| Studying | A specific café or a fancy drink you only buy on study sessions |
| Stretching | An episode of a comfort show |
| Admin and emails | Your favourite snack, only at your weekly admin block |

## Making it stick

1. **Be strict.** If the treat is available anyway, the bundle loses its pull.
2. **Choose something you genuinely crave**, not something you merely like.
3. **Refresh the treat** when it gets stale; novelty fades, as the study showed.
4. **Combine it with a cue and a tiny starting step** so the habit doesn't depend on the bundle alone.

## Key takeaway

Attach an immediate pleasure to a habit with delayed rewards, and keep that pleasure exclusive to the habit. Expect a strong start, and keep the habit's other supports in place for when the novelty wears off.
