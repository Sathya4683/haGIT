# Start tiny: why the two-minute version beats the perfect plan

New habits usually fail on the busy day, the tired day, or the day something unexpected happens. A plan that needs 45 minutes and high energy will lose to those days. A plan that needs two minutes will usually survive them.

## Behaviour = motivation + ability + a prompt

BJ Fogg's behaviour model says a behaviour happens when **motivation**, **ability** and a **prompt** come together at the same moment. Motivation is unreliable: it surges on January 1st and fades by February. Ability is the lever you control. Making a behaviour smaller makes it easier, so it can still happen when motivation is low.

## The two-minute rule

A popular way to apply this, from James Clear's *Atomic Habits*, is to scale a habit down until it takes about two minutes:

| Goal | Two-minute version |
|---|---|
| Read more | Read one page |
| Run three times a week | Put on running shoes and step outside |
| Learn Spanish | Do one flashcard |
| Meditate | Sit and take three slow breaths |
| Write a blog | Write one sentence |

The point is not that two minutes of reading changes your life. The point is that **showing up every day** is what turns an action into an automatic routine. Repetition in a stable context is what builds habit strength, and tiny versions make repetition possible.

## "But two minutes is too little"

It often grows on its own. Once your shoes are on, a walk is easy. Once the book is open, a second page is tempting. But give yourself permission to stop after two minutes. If you always force the long version, your brain learns that starting means a big commitment, and starting gets harder.

## Growing the habit

1. Do the tiny version every day until it feels automatic (it may take weeks; see *How long it really takes to build a habit*).
2. Increase gradually: one page becomes five, one flashcard becomes a deck.
3. If you start skipping, scale back down. Shrinking is not failure; it is maintenance.

## How this looks in haGIT

Make the habit name describe the tiny version ("Read 1 page") and let commit messages capture what you actually did ("read 12 pages, chapter 3"). Your streak rewards showing up, and your commit history shows the growth.

## Key takeaway

Make the habit so small you can't say no, especially on bad days. Consistency builds the habit; intensity can come later.
