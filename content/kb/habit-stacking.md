# Habit stacking: anchor new habits to ones you already have

You already have dozens of rock-solid habits: making coffee, brushing your teeth, unlocking your front door, sitting down at your desk. They happen every day without reminders. Habit stacking borrows that reliability.

## The idea

Habits are cued by context. Psychologists Wendy Wood and Dennis Rünger describe habits as responses triggered by the situations we repeatedly perform them in. A new behaviour has no cue yet, so it depends on remembering, and remembering is unreliable.

An existing routine already *is* a cue. Behaviour scientist BJ Fogg calls this an **anchor**: an established action that you attach a new, tiny behaviour to.

> **After I** [existing habit], **I will** [new tiny habit].

- "After I pour my morning coffee, I will write my top task for the day."
- "After I sit on the train, I will do one language lesson."
- "After I plug in my phone at night, I will read one page."

## Choosing a good anchor

- **It happens every day** (or every time you want the new habit to happen).
- **It happens at a consistent time and place.**
- **It ends cleanly.** "After I finish brushing my teeth" is a clear moment; "while I'm working" is not.
- **It fits the new habit physically.** Flossing after brushing works; doing push-ups after brushing might not.

## Keep the new habit tiny at first

Fogg's method pairs anchors with very small behaviours: one push-up, one sentence, one minute. Small behaviours are easy enough to do even on bad days, which keeps the cue-behaviour link strengthening. Once the stack is automatic, the behaviour can grow naturally.

## Celebrate immediately

Fogg also recommends a small moment of positive emotion right after the new behaviour (a fist pump, a "nice", logging the commit). Feeling good right after an action is part of what wires it in. In haGIT, pushing the commit and seeing the square turn green is a ready-made celebration.

## Building a chain

Once one stack is solid, you can add another link:

1. After I pour my coffee → I open my planner.
2. After I open my planner → I write my top task.
3. After I write my top task → I start a 25-minute focus block.

Add one link at a time. If a chain breaks, it usually breaks at the newest link, so shrink that one.

## Key takeaway

Don't rely on remembering. Find something you already do every day and attach a tiny new behaviour to the moment it ends. Write it as "After I ___, I will ___" and make it your habit's description.
