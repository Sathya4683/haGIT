# Commitment devices and accountability

Present-you wants to build the habit. Tomorrow-you is tired and would rather not. A commitment device is something present-you sets up to make it harder for tomorrow-you to back out.

## Money on the line

Economists Xavier Giné, Dean Karlan and Jonathan Zinman offered about 2,000 smokers in the Philippines a savings account called CARES. Participants deposited money for six months and then took a urine test. If they had quit, they got their money back; if not, it went to charity.

About 11% of smokers offered the account signed up. Smokers who were offered CARES were around **3 percentage points more likely to pass the test at six months** than a control group, and the effect **persisted at a surprise test at twelve months**. At the same time, many people who committed still didn't manage to quit. Commitment helps, but it is not magic, and it works best for people who already want to change.

## Lighter-weight commitments

You don't need to bet money. Commitment can be social or structural:

- **Tell someone specific** what you'll do and by when, and when you'll report back.
- **Find an accountability partner** and exchange weekly numbers. In a large meta-analysis of progress monitoring, effects were stronger when progress was **reported to others or made public**.
- **Book it.** A paid class, a scheduled session with a friend, or a calendar invite creates a cost to skipping.
- **Remove the escape route.** Leave your phone in another room during focus time; only bring workout clothes to work so you go straight to the gym.

## Designing a good commitment

1. **Make it specific and checkable.** "Commit at least five days a week in haGIT" is clear; "try harder" isn't.
2. **Keep the stakes meaningful but not crushing.** Losing a small amount stings; losing a lot can make you avoid the whole thing.
3. **Plan for exceptions.** Agree in advance what counts as a legitimate miss (illness, travel).
4. **Review regularly.** A weekly check-in with your partner is the real engine.

## Accountability with haGIT

Your weekly report email and contribution graph make easy things to share. Screenshot the week, send it to a friend, and ask them to do the same. Pair it with an if-then plan for your weakest day and you'll have both a plan and a reason to follow it.

## Key takeaway

Precommitting, whether with money, a promise or a partner, makes backing out costlier and can improve follow-through. Keep commitments specific, stakes reasonable, and check-ins regular.
