# Plan for your weak days with WOOP

Positive thinking feels good, but on its own it doesn't help much with follow-through. Psychologist Gabriele Oettingen found that combining an optimistic goal with a clear look at what stands in the way works better. Her method is called **WOOP**.

## The four steps

1. **Wish:** What do you want to achieve? Make it challenging but feasible. *"Exercise four times a week this month."*
2. **Outcome:** What is the best result of achieving it? Imagine it vividly for a moment. *"More energy and better sleep."*
3. **Obstacle:** What *inside you* is most likely to get in the way? Be honest. *"After work I feel drained and talk myself out of it."*
4. **Plan:** Turn the obstacle into an if-then plan. *"If I feel drained after work, then I'll change into my gym clothes immediately and go for just 15 minutes."*

The technical name is mental contrasting with implementation intentions (MCII): contrasting the desired future with present obstacles, then linking each obstacle to a planned response.

## What the evidence says

A 2021 meta-analysis by Wang, Wang and Gai combined 21 studies with nearly 16,000 participants. MCII improved goal attainment with a **small-to-medium effect (g = 0.34)**. Interventions delivered through interaction with a person worked better than those delivered only through written materials. It is a modest but reliable boost, and it takes only a few minutes.

## Why the obstacle step matters

Most habit plans are designed for an average day. Habits break on the *weak* days: the late meeting, the bad night's sleep, the rainy Friday. WOOP makes you plan for those days in advance, so you're not improvising when your resolve is lowest.

## Using WOOP with your data

Your habit history is a map of your obstacles. Look at it with fresh eyes:

- **Weak weekday?** Write a WOOP for that day specifically.
- **Habit going quiet?** Ask what changed in your routine and plan around it.
- **Evening slump?** Move the habit earlier or create an if-then plan for the slump.

The haGIT coach can point out your weakest weekday and recent trends; turn each "watch-out" it gives you into an Obstacle and a Plan.

## A five-minute WOOP routine

Once a week, perhaps during your weekly review:

1. Pick one habit.
2. Write the Wish and Outcome in a sentence each.
3. Name the single most likely Obstacle for the coming week.
4. Write one "If [obstacle], then I will [action]" plan.

## Key takeaway

Dream, then get real. Picture the outcome, name your main inner obstacle honestly, and pre-plan exactly what you'll do when it shows up.
